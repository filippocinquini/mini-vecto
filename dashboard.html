TRA545 Hydrogen Technologies · heavy-duty powertrain simulator

Five powertrains on the same mission, every 10 metres.

mini-VECTO drives one vehicle along a mission and works back from the speed it must keep to the fuel, hydrogen or electricity it needs. Diesel, HVO100, hydrogen engine, fuel cell and battery-electric all drive exactly the same cycle, so the differences below come from the powertrain alone.

How the model works

A backward simulation in five steps, repeated every 10 m of the mission. Each step opens the view that shows it.

RouteSpeed limits, curves, grade and stops along the mission (VECTO cycles for the buses). DriverBrakes in time and accelerates as far as driver and power allow: the speed really driven. Wheel forceInertia + grade + rolling + air drag, with each powertrain's own mass. PowertrainGearbox and engine map, or e-motor with battery and fuel cell. EnergyFuel, hydrogen or electricity per step, summed per mission and exported to Excel.

What happens in these steps

Route. missions.py describes the mission along distance: speed limit, curve radius, grade, surface and stops. Each curve lowers the limit so that the lateral acceleration v²/R stays within what drivers accept (Trafikverket). The buses run on the VECTO Urban and Coach cycles.

Driver. A backward sweep from the end makes the vehicle brake in time for every limit and stop (v² = v_next² + 2·d·Δs). A forward sweep accelerates as the driver wishes and the engine power allows. The cycle is built once and every powertrain drives it.

Wheel force. F = m_eff·a + m·g·sin α + c_r·m·g·cos α + ½·ρ·C_dA·v². Battery and hydrogen tanks add mass, so the same cycle needs more force. Negative force is braking: an electric drive recovers part of it, an engine wastes it in the brakes.

Where the losses come from

Copper losses grow with the square of the torque (current through the windings): the motor is least efficient at high torque and low speed, as when a bus pulls away. Iron losses grow with speed (magnetising the core) and friction and windage with the cube of speed. The inverter adds switching and conduction losses. The best efficiency sits in the middle, at moderate torque and speed.

Speed is shown per unit of the base speed, the speed above which the motor can no longer give full torque and runs at constant power (the curved limit).

Fuel cell

The fuel cell system turns hydrogen into electricity with an efficiency that depends on load. It is most efficient at part load, so the buffer battery takes the peaks and lets the fuel cell run smoothly.

Why the efficiency curve has this shape

At high load the cell voltage drops (activation, ohmic and mass-transport losses grow with current), so each kilowatt costs more hydrogen. At very low load the air compressor and the other auxiliaries of the system eat a large share of a small output. The peak of the system curve therefore sits at about a quarter of rated power. Ageing lowers the whole curve: the model gives every result for a new and an aged stack.

Curve points at 25 % and 100 % load: DOE Program Record 26002 (new) and Wang & Ahluwalia 2025 (aged); the points in between are interpolated (assumption).
